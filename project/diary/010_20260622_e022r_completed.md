## Key findings

### Finding 1: E022 single-seed result was a high-end draw

E022 used DEFAULT_SEED=42 → shuffled rng seed=119, which produced gSIG-A = 0.091.
With 20 seeds, the mean is 0.0091 — ten times lower. The E022 single value was
approximately 1.8 SDs above the 20-seed mean, which is the expected high tail for
one draw from a distribution with SD ≈ 0.046.

The E022 "full_structural_match" classification for shuffled_replay canonical was
therefore a single-seed artefact. E022R confirms it.

### Finding 2: The ratio decays rapidly and reaches noise floor by n=16

- n=4: ratio ≈ 0.07 (7% of full model; not zero, but small and noisy)
- n=8: ratio ≈ 0.04 (4%)
- n=16: ratio ≈ 0.001 (essentially zero — within measurement noise)
- n=32: ratio ≈ 0.001

The SD also decreases monotonically (0.046 → 0.031 → 0.013 → 0.005), confirming that
variance in the shuffled gSIG-A reduces as branch count increases.

### Finding 3: Full model gSIG-A increases with branch count

The full model's gSIG-A grows from 0.129 (n=4) to 0.295 (n=32). This makes sense:
with more private branches, each trace writes more selectively to its overlap branch,
increasing the difference between overlap and non-overlap branch updates.

### Finding 4: The sampling interpretation is mechanistically correct

At n=4 branches with 1 overlap branch, random allocation assigns probability 1/4 to
the overlap branch per weight entry. Over 9 consolidation passes, the overlap branch
receives elevated M_b by chance ≈7% as often as the full model. By n=16, the
probability drops to 1/16 per weight entry and the signal averages out completely.

## Confirmed article claim

> Shuffled replay can partially mimic overlap-branch writing in the smallest four-branch
> motif because random reassignment has a high chance of revisiting the same branch
> (probability 1/n_branches per allocation weight). This apparent match decays as branch
> allocation space increases, supporting the interpretation that identity-preserving
> replay, not replay alone, is required for scalable structural specificity.
