## Key findings

### 1. Canonical behavior reproduces across 4–32 branches (4/4 pass)

gSIG-A scales from +0.129 (n=4) to +0.295 (n=32). The increase reflects that added neutral
branches dilute non-overlap mean M_b, making the overlap branch advantage larger.
gSIG-B and gSIG-E remain stable: mechanism is branch-count invariant.

### 2. Weak overlap correctly fails (0/4 pass)

With overlap_weight=0.30 (below E020 threshold of ~0.40), the overlap branch cannot accumulate
sufficient structural accessibility to drive linking. gSIG-A becomes negative (non-overlap
branches write MORE than the underpowered overlap branch). This confirms E020's boundary finding.

### 3. Strong overlap passes at all branch counts (4/4 pass)

gSIG-B is slightly larger than canonical (+0.295 vs +0.239), confirming that stronger overlap
produces more specific linking.

### 4. Chain overlap passes with clear local > distant linking (3/3 pass; FL ≈ 0.19)

False-linking rate ≈ 0.19 means distant (unlinked) pairs gain ~19% as much linking as
expected local pairs. This is a valid and expected boundary: chain topology produces
mostly local linking but with non-zero cross-contamination through the intermediate trace.

### 5. Hub overlap passes, but false-linking is universal (3/3 pass; FL = N/A)

Hub motif passes gSIG-A, B, D, E because all traces write strongly to the hub branch and
all pairs are "expected linked." However, false-linking_rate is NaN (undefined, since
all pairs ARE expected to link). This is a key article-facing finding: hub motif produces
strong linking but poor specificity — ALL traces become linked regardless of whether
they were encoded together.

### 6. Sparse random passes at both seeds and all branch counts (6/6 pass)

False-linking rates (0.16–0.30) are modest, indicating that random sparse allocation provides
reasonable specificity. At n=32, gSIG-B rises to +0.60 (due to more unique private branches
reducing noise in unlinked pairs as n grows).
