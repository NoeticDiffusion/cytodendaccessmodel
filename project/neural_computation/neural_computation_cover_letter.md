Dear Editors,

Please consider *Testing Slow Branch-Level Accessibility in a Simulator of Memory Linking* for publication as a full-length research article in *Neural Computation*.

The manuscript studies a computational hypothesis: whether a phenomenological, branch-specific slow-accessibility state adds predictive value for memory linking beyond matched weight-based, resource-based, replay, eligibility, and gain alternatives. We formalize the state in an executable branch-resolved simulator and compare ten simulator families under a shared cue, probe, and output measure, with equal candidate budgets and a locked holdout of generated topologies.

The comparison gives a bounded result rather than a claim of unique advantage. The tuned full model exceeds five qualifying alternatives, is practically equivalent to shuffled replay, and is outperformed on the primary readout by Hebbian weight-only and resource-only models. Eligibility-only yields no qualifying candidate under the registered filters. We therefore do not claim that slow branch writing is necessary, that the full model is uniquely superior, or that the latent variable identifies a biological storage substrate. We present the general link between branch-local plasticity and memory linking as prior context; the contribution here is the explicit model and the outcome-level comparison of what this implementation does and does not distinguish.

This focus on mechanistic modeling, computational neuroscience, and model discrimination fits the journal's scope in theory, modeling, and computation in neuroscience.
