## Main weakness

# Major issues to fix

## 1. The introduction is too short and under-referenced

The introduction currently states the problem clearly, but it does not yet sufficiently situate the work in the literature. A reviewer will want to know exactly how this relates to:

* dendritic computation,
* synaptic tagging and capture,
* local translation,
* engram / memory linking,
* replay consolidation,
* contextual gating,
* associative memory models,
* dendritic allocation,
* structural plasticity,
* cytoskeletal / transport / metabolic candidate mechanisms.

Right now the introduction is conceptually good but too light. It needs to show that the author understands the field and that the simulator is addressing a precise gap.

### Fix

Expand the introduction into 5–6 paragraphs:

1. Classical associative memory and recurrent/synaptic accounts.
2. Dendrites and spines as active access-control compartments.
3. STC/local translation/replay as slow stabilization mechanisms.
4. Memory linking and branch/segment reuse.
5. The gap: no minimal executable test separating fast gating, fixed overlap, generic drift, weight-only learning, and slow branch writing.
6. Present study.

Do not make it a long review. But it needs enough external grounding to avoid looking self-contained.

## 6. Avoid “Article-facing claim” and “Boundary” labels in the manuscript

These are excellent internal writing tools, but they should not appear as repeated labels in the final paper.

They make the manuscript feel like a draft or grant memo.

### Fix

Convert each pair into normal prose.

Instead of:

> Article-facing claim: The simulator emits reproducible branch-level and trace-level dynamics.
> Boundary: This is an internal simulator validation, not biological validation.

Write:

> These traces establish that the model’s internal branch-level dynamics are inspectable and reproducible. This result validates the simulator implementation, but it does not constitute biological validation.

You can keep a single **“Interpretive boundaries”** subsection in Discussion, but not after every Result.

## 7. The comparator argument is strong but vulnerable to circularity

This is the biggest scientific-review risk.

A reviewer may say:

> Of course comparators without (M_b) fail (M_b)-based signatures.

The draft partially solves this by adding behavioral-output metrics, but this needs to be made even more explicit and earlier.

### Fix

In the comparator Results section, clearly separate:

**Mechanistic signatures:**
SIG-A / gSIG-A, structural write advantage, rescue of written (M_b).

**Output-level signatures:**
linking gain, recall support, damage sensitivity, recovery, false-linking.

Then state:

> The comparator analysis was not designed to declare any model without (M_b) a failure by definition. Instead, comparators were first evaluated for output-level equivalence and then for whether they reproduced the proposed structural-access mechanism.

This is important.

## 8. “Behavioral” may be misleading

The draft says “behavioral-output metrics.” But these are simulator outputs, not animal behavior.

A strict reviewer may object.

### Fix

Use:

* **output-level metrics**
* **simulator-output metrics**
* **readout-level metrics**

Avoid “behavioral” unless you explicitly write “simulated behavioral proxy.”

Recommended phrase:

> output-level linking and recall metrics

## 9. The biological interpretation is still too late and too thin

The paper now avoids overclaiming, which is good. But it may undersell biological relevance. The Discussion says (M_b) may represent spine geometry, actin, microtubule entry, local translation, transport readiness, mitochondrial or metabolic support. That is good, but the main text needs a stronger bridge before Results.

Add a subsection before the model:

Make it clear that (M_b) is not invented arbitrarily. It compresses a plausible family of slow branch-local processes.

## 12. The abstract is good but slightly overloaded

The abstract is strong, but it lists too many comparators in one sentence:

> fast-gating-only, replay-without-structure, random-drift, fixed-allocation, weight-only, global-gain, shuffled-replay, eligibility-only, or resource-only comparators

This is impressive but heavy.

### Fix

Shorten:

> ...not reproduced by fast-gating, fixed-allocation, random-drift, weight-only, global-gain, shuffled-replay, eligibility-only, or resource-only comparators.

Or:

> ...not reproduced by nine simpler or alternative comparator classes.

Then name them later.

Also add one phrase that this is a **computational model**, not just “framework.”
