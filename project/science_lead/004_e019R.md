# E019R — Signature Protocol Lock and Rescue Audit

## Purpose

Lock the canonical signature computation protocol before running two-parameter heatmaps.

E017 established trace export.
E018 established comparator discrimination.
E019 established one-at-a-time robustness.

However, E019 revealed one issue that must be resolved before E020:

```text
SIG-E canonical magnitude differs substantially between E018 and E019.
E018: approximately +33.3 pp
E019: approximately +154.7 pp
```

This may reflect a valid rescue-overshoot effect, a unit-labeling problem, a protocol difference, or a metric-normalization issue. E019R should determine which.

The goal is not to change the science to make the result prettier. The goal is to make the signature definitions auditable, stable, and manuscript-safe.

---

## Core research question

Are SIG-A to SIG-E computed identically and interpreted consistently across E017, E018, E019, and the planned E020 pipeline?

---

## Required tasks

### 1. Centralize signature computation

Create or refactor a single shared signature-computation module, for example:

```text
src/cytodend_accessmodel/signatures.py
```

This module should expose one canonical function:

```text
compute_signature_profile(run_outputs, protocol_config) -> SignatureProfile
```

or equivalent.

E017, E018, E019, and future E020 should use this shared function rather than independently reimplementing signature logic.

### 3. Audit SIG-E

Produce a dedicated SIG-E audit.

Compare at least these rescue conditions under the full model:

```text
no_rescue
targeted_overlap_rescue
generic_plain_consolidation_rescue
generic_all_branch_precue_rescue
nonoverlap_branch_rescue
```

For each condition, export:

```text
L_pre_damage
L_post_damage
L_post_rescue
absolute_recovery
fractional_recovery
targeted_advantage_raw
targeted_advantage_normalized
```

Write:

```text
results/e019r_signature_protocol_lock/summary/sig_e_rescue_audit.csv
results/e019r_signature_protocol_lock/summary/sig_e_interpretation.md
```

Important unit rule:

```text
Do not call SIG-E “percentage points” unless the underlying quantity is bounded
and explicitly scaled between 0 and 1.
```

If the linking score is unbounded or can overshoot baseline, report SIG-E as:

```text
raw linking-support difference
```

or use a normalized recovery index:

```text
normalized_recovery = (L_post_rescue - L_post_damage) / (L_pre_damage - L_post_damage)
```

If normalized recovery can exceed 1.0, call it “overshoot” rather than “percentage points.”

### 5. Decide final article-facing signature language

Create:

```text
results/e019r_signature_protocol_lock/summary/article_signature_language.md
```

Recommended likely language:

```text
SIG-A and SIG-B are slow-writing-dependent signatures.
SIG-C is an architectural context/allocation separation signature and is not diagnostic of slow writing.
SIG-D is an overlap-perturbation sensitivity signature and is not diagnostic alone.
SIG-E is a rescue-selectivity signature, but its magnitude depends on the rescue protocol and should be reported with raw and normalized values.
The paper’s model-discrimination claim rests on the joint profile, especially the combination of SIG-A, SIG-B, and SIG-E, rather than on any single signature.
```

3. SIG-E rescue protocols are audited.
4. Canonical rows from E017/E018/E019 are reproduced or differences are explained.
5. Unit labels are corrected.
6. Article-facing signature language is drafted.
7. Tests pass.
8. E020 can safely reuse the locked signature protocol.
