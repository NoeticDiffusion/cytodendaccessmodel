Dear Dr Langell,

Thank you for submitting your paper, "A branch-resolved cytoskeletal-dendritic accessibility model of associative memory", to eLife. Your submission has been considered by the editors. Since we receive more submissions than our editors can handle, we prioritise ones for which we can produce high-quality public reviews that will be of significant value to the community.

Unfortunately in this case we are unable to proceed as we believe the submission would be better served being peer reviewed within a more specialized community.

We hope the following comments will be useful to you:

The core concerns identified by the reviewing editors are as follows. First, the conceptual framing is weak: the link between the proposed simulator and the structural hypothesis it purports to test is insufficiently developed, and key terms in the abstract such as notably "memory linking" and "simpler comparator baselines" are undefined, undermining the paper's accessibility and rigour. Second, the empirical evidence is unconvincing: effect sizes are small, the selected datasets are inadequately characterised, and no alternative datasets are evaluated for robustness. The absence of simulation traces or experimental figures is a major limitation for a methods-oriented contribution. Third, the technical derivations are problematic and very hard to follow. Taken together, these issues reflect concerns about the rigor, soundness and clarity of the work. It is likely that a number of datasets in the DANDI repository show marginal support for the hypothesis as encoded in an abstracted simulator. However such a finding does not meet eLife's standards for impact or evidential rigour.

We appreciate your interest in a public review process: other services that offer public reviews include Review Commons (https://www.reviewcommons.org/) and Peer Community In (https://peercommunityin.org/). We wish you good luck with this work and we hope you will consider eLife for future submissions.

Best wishes,
